ImageClearinghouse

Use this table to locate or create high-quality, legally reusable images. All listed resources offer copyright-flexible or public domain content, but you must verify license details for each image before use.

Start Here

Always start by considering whether your use is educational (for school), commercial (for profit), or transformative (for artistic purposes). Understanding copyright basics protects you and respects creators' rights.

Warning

While most of these images are safe for school, it's impossible to guarantee that each site is completely free of content you may consider objectionable. By using this guide, you agree to exhibit maturity and exercise discretion.

Common Licenses at a Glance

Public Domain
No restrictions. Use freely without attribution (though attribution is both polite and good scholarly practice).
CC0
Creator waives all rights. No attribution required, but recommended.
CC BY
Credit the creator. Commercial use allowed.
CC BY-NC
Credit the creator. Non-commercial use only.
CC BY-NC-ND
Credit the creator. Non-commercial use only. No modifications allowed.
CC BY-SA
Credit the creator. Share adaptations under the same license.

How to Cite Images

MLA 9th Edition

In MLA format, visuals should be labeled Figure (abbreviated Fig.), assigned an Arabic numeral, and accompanied by a descriptive caption. General Works Cited format is as follows (remember to add a hanging indent!):

Creator's Last Name, First Name. Title of Image. Date (Month Day, Year). Title of Website, URL. Access date optional.

If you're using a digital image embedded in an article, place the title in quotation marks.

If you choose to include the full citation in your caption, don't repeat it on your Works Cited page.

Chicago 18th Edition (Notes-Bibliography)

In Chicago, captions can be labeled Figure (abbreviated Fig.) or Illustration (abbreviated Ill.), but must be assigned an Arabic numeral. Bibliography format is as follows (remember to add a hanging indent!):

Creator's Last Name, First Name. Title of Image. Date (Month Day, Year). Medium and dimensions (if relevant, say, for a painting). Accession number (if available). Title of Website. URL.

Ethics and AI Images

AI image generators are trained on large datasets that almost definitely include copyrighted works. While you may own the outputs, consider the ethical implications of using AI-generated content in academic work. Always disclose when images are AI-generated and consult your teacher before using them for assignments. Check this doc for information on citing generative AI.
